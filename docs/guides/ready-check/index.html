Inhouse League · Lobby Systems

How Ready Check Works

Ready

You're in the lobby's voice channel, or your Discord status is Online or Do Not Disturb.

Playing

Discord shows you as currently playing Dota 2.

Recent

You joined the lobby less than 10 minutes ago — benefit of the doubt.

Possibly AFK

None of the above. Idle, no voice, no game activity, joined a while ago.

The bot assigns you one of these four labels automatically, every time it looks at the lobby — you never opt in or out of the label. Everything below builds on it.

01

Starting a check

Anyone currently in the lobby can run /readycheck. It posts a message in the lobby thread listing everyone in three groups: Likely Active (Ready, Playing, or Recent), Possibly AFK, and Reacted to Ready Check once people start confirming.

You can only run it once every 2 minutes per lobby — running it again sooner just tells you to wait.

Pending merge A branch not yet on main adds a floor: below 8 players in the lobby, /readycheck refuses to start at all and just replies with how many more are needed. This is separate from the 10-player quorum in section 05 below — 8 gets you a check, 10 gets you the "go shuffle" announcement.
02

Getting counted without lifting a finger

Two groups are marked confirmed the instant the check is posted, with no reaction required:

  • Whoever ran the command (if they chose to confirm themselves when running it)
  • Anyone labeled Recent — you joined less than 10 minutes ago, so the bot assumes you're still paying attention

Everyone else has to physically react with ✅ to be counted.

03

Confirming, or backing out

React with ✅ on the message to confirm. Remove your reaction and you go back to unconfirmed — and the bot remembers you explicitly opted out, so a later roster refresh won't silently re-confirm you just because you still look "Recent."

04

Staying in sync while it's live

The ready check message updates itself in real time as the lobby changes — nobody has to re-run the command. If someone joins, leaves, gets kicked, gets suspended, or gets swept out for a curfew window, the roster and the ✅ counts update immediately, and any reaction they left behind is removed.

05

Reaching quorum

Once enough players are confirmed — 10 by default, though a server can configure a different number — the bot posts one announcement that /shuffle is available. Extra players beyond that number are never required to confirm at all.

06

The 30-minute sweep

This is the part that surprises people. If a ready check has been sitting for 30 minutes or more and someone runs /readycheck again, the bot treats it as stale, deletes it, and posts a fresh one — but first, it quietly removes every player who meets all of these conditions:

  • Currently labeled Possibly AFK
  • Joined the lobby more than 10 minutes ago
  • Never reacted ✅
  • Isn't the person running the command
  • Isn't already locked into a forming match

They're removed from the lobby outright, not just marked unconfirmed. There is no DM. There is no message in the thread naming them. The only trace is a private note shown to whoever ran the command — "Removed 2 away player(s); they can rejoin with /join." — which doesn't even say who.

For discussion

Where we want your opinion

Everything above is what the bot does today, not what it should do. These are the specific calls worth putting to a vote — reference them by number.

1

Silent removal, no notification

A swept player finds out only by noticing they're no longer in the lobby. Should they get a DM explaining why, the way a curfew removal or a kick already does?

Today: nothing sent to the removed player at all.

2

No warning before it happens

The removal happens the moment someone re-runs the check past 30 minutes — there's no "you'll be dropped in 5 minutes, react now" heads-up first.

Today: instant, no grace period.

3

What actually counts as "AFK"

The AFK guess is built from voice presence, Online/DND status, and a detected "Dota 2" activity. It has no way to see mobile status, a deliberately-set Invisible/DND, or someone tabbed out reading a guide. Are these the right signals, or too blunt?

Today: three signals, all-or-nothing.

4

The two timers

A join is "Recent" for 10 minutes. A ready check goes stale after 30. Both are fixed and apply to every lobby. Do either need to change?

Today: 10-minute grace, 30-minute staleness.

5

The 2-minute cooldown

Nobody can re-post a check for 2 minutes after the last one, even to fix a mistake or chase a straggler. Right length?

Today: 2 minutes, per lobby, no exceptions.

6

Extras never have to confirm

Once the quorum is hit, anyone still in the lobby beyond that number is never asked to react at all — they just ride along. Should overflow players still have to confirm, in case the shuffle needs to swap someone in?

Today: only the first N confirmations matter.

7

Who gets pinged, and when

A brand-new check doesn't ping people who just joined (they're assumed to know). But refreshing an existing check pings everyone still unconfirmed, recent joiners included. Should that be consistent one way or the other?

Today: new checks and refreshes ping differently.

8
New, not yet merged

Is 8 the right floor to even start a check?

A check below 8 players now gets rejected outright — not "posted with fewer confirmations required," just refused, with a reply telling you the shortfall. 8 was picked as a reasonable-sounding number, not something the group weighed in on. Is it right? Should it scale with the 10-player quorum instead of sitting at a fixed number, and should it eventually be a per-server setting the way quorum already is?

Today: fixed at 8, hardcoded, no admin control.

Reply in the Discord guides thread quoting the number of the point you're answering — that's the fastest way to get it into the next revision. Reflects /readycheck behavior on cama-apps/cama-mm main, plus one change (marked Pending merge) that's on an open branch but not merged yet.
